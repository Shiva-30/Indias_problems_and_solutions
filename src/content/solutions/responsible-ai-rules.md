## How it works

1. AI uses are sorted by risk into prohibited, high-risk and transparency categories.[^eu]
2. The EU bans uses such as social scoring, manipulating people, real-time biometric identification in public spaces and scraping images to build face recognition databases.[^eu]
3. Breaking the rules can cost up to €35 million or 7% of global turnover.[^eu]

## Evidence so far

- The EU AI Act entered into force on August 1, 2024, and its bans applied from February 2, 2025.[^eu]
- UNESCO's 193 member states adopted a shared recommendation on AI ethics in November 2021, and UNESCO asks states to report on progress.[^unesco]
- India's AI governance guidelines of November 2025 start with voluntary commitments and amendments to existing laws, not a dedicated AI law.[^india]
