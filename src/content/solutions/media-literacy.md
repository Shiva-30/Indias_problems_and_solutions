## How it works

1. Students take four 90-minute sessions over about 14 weeks, with homework in between.[^vox]
2. They learn to check sources, spot emotional manipulation and verify claims, and to share and correct information responsibly.[^vox]
3. Role-plays let them practise, for example questioning a parent who shares a doubtful health rumour.[^vox]

## Evidence so far

- In a randomised trial with about 13,500 students aged 13 to 18 at 100 community libraries in Bihar, students got clearly better at telling true health news from false and became much less likely to share false stories.[^vox]
- The gains held four months later, carried over to political news that was never taught, and spread to students' parents.[^vox]

[^vox]: Amar, Badrinathan, Chauchard and Sichart, [Countering misinformation in the classroom](https://voxdev.org/topic/education/countering-misinformation-classroom), VoxDev, January 2026.
