## How it works

1. Candidates file a sworn affidavit with their nomination papers, covering criminal cases, income, assets and education.[^legacy]
2. Since a 2018 Supreme Court ruling, candidates and parties must publish a candidate's criminal history at least three times before an election, in local newspapers and on electronic media.[^legacy]
3. Giving false information or hiding details can bring up to six months in jail, a fine or both.[^legacy]
4. The Association for Democratic Reforms analyses the affidavits and publishes findings for voters.[^adr]

## Evidence so far

- Disclosure has not stopped candidates with criminal cases from winning: 46% of MPs elected in 2024 declared criminal cases.[^adr]
- Some candidates have filed incomplete affidavits, and the Law Commission and Election Commission have asked for tougher penalties for false ones.[^legacy]
