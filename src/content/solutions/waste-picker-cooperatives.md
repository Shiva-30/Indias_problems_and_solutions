## How it works

1. Waste pickers buy shares in the cooperative and work in pairs, each pair serving 150 to 400 households.[^wri]
2. They earn from user fees paid by households and by selling recyclables. The city pays for slum households.[^wri]
3. SWaCH has two long-term contracts with the Pune Municipal Corporation and negotiates safety standards, equipment and health care.[^wri]

## Evidence so far

- SWaCH handles about 1,000 tonnes of waste a day, including in slums that had no collection before.[^wri]
- SWaCH estimates its user-fee model saved the city about $13 million in one year compared with tax-funded collection.[^wri]

[^wri]: [Urban transformations: in Pune, India, waste pickers go from trash to treasure](https://www.wri.org/insights/urban-transformations-pune-india-waste-pickers-go-trash-treasure), World Resources Institute, March 2019.
