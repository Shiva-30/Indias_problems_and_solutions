## Causes

- Sons are expected to carry the family name, support parents in old age, inherit land and, especially among Hindus, perform funeral rites, while dowry makes daughters look like a cost.[^son]
- Family honour is tied to how women behave, so families restrict where women go. In villages where few women veil, families that grow richer often restrict women more, to signal higher status.[^veil]
- Seclusion of women (purdah or ghoonghat) cuts across religions. A burqa or headscarf is most common among Muslims, and covering the face with a sari or dupatta among Hindus and others. Both bring similar restrictions.[^veil]

## Impact

- Son preference among married women ranges from 8.2% to 42.3% across districts, and is highest in northern and central India.[^son]
- Purdah and ghoonghat restrict where women can go, which makes it harder for them to work outside the home.[^purdah]
- In north Karnataka, fear over girls' "purity" after puberty led families to restrict their movement, and some girls dropped out of school.[^school]
- Women of different religions in Jammu and Kashmir are still kept apart during menstruation, which harms their emotional and physical wellbeing.[^mens]
- Other customs treat women unequally too: dowry, instant triple talaq and cutting girls' genitals (khatna). See [Celebrating without harm](/celebrations/) for these and more, with sources.[^talaq][^fgm]
